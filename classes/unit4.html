Argument Planning

Provide evidence for your ideas, but also think about what the other person might say.

Be prepared to acknowledge and refute their ideas.

Me -I believe we should stop investing money to go to space, instead we should invest it on Earth, what is your opinion on that topic?

ChatGPT -I think your position is reasonable, especially if the goal is to improve people's lives right now. There are still major problems on Earth—poverty, climate change, lack of access to education and healthcare, pollution, and food and water insecurity—so investing more in these areas could have a direct impact.

However, I wouldn't completely stop investing in space. Space research has produced useful technologies, including improvements in communications, weather forecasting, navigation, and Earth observation. Satellites, for example, help monitor climate change and natural disasters.

So, my opinion is that the priority should be Earth, but space research shouldn't be abandoned. Governments could reduce spending on expensive space projects whose benefits are mainly exploratory and put more money into solving urgent problems on Earth, while continuing space programs that provide scientific knowledge or practical benefits.

A balanced position could be: “We shouldn't stop exploring space, but we should make sure that our planet's most urgent problems come first.”

Me -I see your point, but I believe it's an good idea to stop entirely for some time until global problems like: climate, wars and global pollution gets stable enough to be able to focus in space travel

ChatGPT -I understand your argument, and I think it becomes stronger if you frame it as a temporary pause rather than permanently abandoning space exploration.

The main question I'd raise is: how do we decide when the problems are “stable enough”? Climate change, wars, and pollution may never reach a point where they are completely solved. If we wait for perfection, humanity might never return to space.

But I agree with the principle behind your idea: when resources are limited, governments should prioritize urgent problems that directly affect billions of people. We could temporarily reduce funding for ambitious space-travel projects while maintaining only the space programs that are especially useful on Earth, such as satellites for weather, communications, climate monitoring, and disaster prevention.

So I'd put our positions together like this

Humanity shouldn't abandon space forever, but during periods of severe global crises, solving urgent problems on Earth should take priority. Once humanity has made substantial progress toward those problems, investment in space exploration could gradually increase again.

That's actually a pretty defensible middle ground: Earth first, space later—but not space never.
